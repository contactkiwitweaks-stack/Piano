# Harmonic Grammar & Voice Leading: The Chopin & Liszt Syntax

## 1. Introduction to Romantic Harmonic Syntax
In Classical harmony (Bach, Mozart, Beethoven), harmonic movement is fundamentally architectural and functional: Tonic establishes home, Subdominant provides motion, Dominant creates directional tension, and Tonic resolves.

Chopin and Franz Liszt radicalized this system by treating harmony not merely as functional punctuation, but as **chromatic color, psychological atmosphere, and emotional state**. They unchained chords from diatonic constraints through:
1. Linear chromatic voice leading (harmony generated as the consequence of independent voices moving by semitones).
2. Distant third-relations (chromatic mediants).
3. Enharmonic ambiguity (diminished seventh and augmented triad reinterpretation).
4. Prolonged non-resolutions and suspended cadences.

This treatise codifies the specific harmonic progressions, chord voicings, and voice-leading formulas utilized in our original masterpiece **"Sorrow"** in G minor.

## 2. The Core Harmonic Catalog

### 2.1 The Neapolitan Sixth (N6) & Chromatic Subdominant
- **Definition**: A major triad built on the lowered second scale degree (bII), typically voiced in first inversion (N6).
- **In G Minor**: The root is Ab Major (Ab - C - Eb). In first inversion, the bass is C (scale degree 4), creating:
  - Bass: C3
  - Tenor: Ab3
  - Alto: Eb4
  - Soprano: C5 or Ab4
- **Voice Leading Resolution**:
  - The lowered second degree (Ab) possesses an intense downward gravity, resolving down by semitone to the leading tone (F#) or scale degree 7, or passing through a diminished seventh chord (F# dim 7).
  - The fifth of the Neapolitan chord (Eb) resolves down by half-step to D (scale degree 5).
  - The third (C) can move down to Bb or remain on C to form a 4–3 suspension over the dominant D7 chord (D - F# - A - C).
- **Emotional Character in Chopin**: Expresses sudden unbearable sorrow, tragic resignation, and pale weeping beauty.

### 2.2 The Augmented Sixth Chords (Italian, French, German)
Augmented sixth chords are built by expanding scale degree b6 (Eb in G minor) and raised scale degree #4 (C# in G minor) outward by contrary motion to an octave on the dominant (D - D).

1. **Italian Sixth (It+6)**:
   - Notes: Eb - G - C#
   - Resolution: Eb moves down to D; C# moves up to D; G resolves to F# (dominant third).
   - Character: Pungent, stark, immediate cry of grief.
2. **French Sixth (Fr+6)**:
   - Notes: Eb - G - A - C# (contains whole-tone flavor via the A).
   - Resolution: Both outer voices resolve outward to D; the whole-tone ambiguity creates an eerie, suspended acoustic quality.
3. **German Sixth (Ger+6)**:
   - Notes: Eb - G - Bb - C#
   - Resolution: Eb and C# resolve outward to D; to avoid parallel fifths between Eb-Bb and D-A, it typically passes through the *cadential six-four* chord (G minor over D: D - G - Bb - D) before resolving to the dominant D7!
   - Character: Massive, dark, visceral tragic power.

### 2.3 Chromatic Mediants & Third-Relations
Chopin and Liszt frequently bypassed the traditional circle of fifths, leaping instead to chords whose roots lie a third away (major or minor third) sharing only one or zero common tones:
- **From G minor (Tonic)**:
  - **Eb Major (bVI)**: Submediant. Warm, nostalgic oasis. Common tones: G and Bb.
  - **B Major (Chromatic Major Mediant, VI#)**: Root is B natural (raised third relative to G). Common tones: zero! The transition from G minor to B major feels like waking inside an otherworldly, golden dream (used by Liszt in Liebestraum and Chopin in the F minor Ballade).
  - **Bb minor (Minor Mediant, v of III)**: Dark, shadowy depression of the expected relative major.
  - **E Major (Submediant with raised 6th)**: Luminous, ecstatic burst of romantic longing.

### 2.4 Diminished Seventh Enharmonic Pivots (The Lisztian Crossroads)
Because a diminished seventh chord divides the 12-semitone octave into four equal minor thirds (3 + 3 + 3 + 3), any diminished seventh chord can be spelled enharmonically in four different ways, serving as the leading-tone chord to four different major or minor keys separated by minor thirds!
- **Example: F# - A - C - Eb**:
  1. Spelled F# - A - C - Eb: Resolves to **G minor** (or G Major).
  2. Spelled D# - F# - A - C: Resolves to **E minor** (or E Major).
  3. Spelled B# - D# - F# - A: Resolves to **C# minor** (or C# Major).
  4. Spelled A - C - Eb - Gb: Resolves to **Bb minor** (or Bb Major).
- **Application**: Liszt utilizes this symmetry to create rapid, dizzying modulations where the tonal gravity seems to vanish mid-phrase, before anchoring thunderously in an unexpected key.

## 3. Voice Leading Rules for High Romantic Polyphony

### Rule 1: Contrary Motion Outer Frame
- Whenever the soprano melody makes an expressive upward leap (e.g. rising from D4 to Bb5), the bass octave MUST move in contrary downward motion (e.g. descending from G2 to Eb1).
- This opens the acoustic soundboard of the piano, creating a vast resonance space for the middle-register arpeggios to bloom without clutter.

### Rule 2: Strict Avoidance of Parallel Fifths in Inner Voices
- Even in dense 8-note chord voicings, parallel fifths and parallel octaves between active voice strands sound hollow and amateurish.
- Use oblique motion (holding one voice stationary while another moves) or contrary voice crossing to preserve lush polyphonic independence.

### Rule 3: The Weeping Chromatic Line (Passus Duriusculus)
- The historic lament bass: descending chromatically from Tonic to Dominant:
  `G -> F# -> F natural -> E natural -> Eb -> D`
- Voiced across successive measures:
  - Bar 1: G minor (G in bass)
  - Bar 2: D7/F# (First inversion dominant, F# in bass)
  - Bar 3: G min 7 / F natural (F natural in bass)
  - Bar 4: C7 / E natural (Applied dominant to Neapolitan, E natural in bass)
  - Bar 5: Eb Major / C minor 6 (Eb in bass)
  - Bar 6: D sus 4 -> D Major 7 (Dominant arrival on D)
- This 6-bar chromatic descent is the harmonic backbone of the *Moderato doloroso* opening of "Sorrow".

## 4. The Harmonic Climax: The Neapolitan-Augmented Chain
In the volcanic climax preceding the Coda:
1. G minor fortissimo downbeat (Tonic foundation).
2. Leaping immediately to Ab Major / C (Neapolitan 6th, *ff*).
3. Chromatic contraction: Bass rises from C to C#, inner voice holds Eb, soprano leaps to high G: **German Augmented Sixth (Ger+6)**!
4. Explosion into D Major cadential six-four (*fff*).
5. Tremolo trill on dominant 7th with flat ninth (D - F# - A - C - Eb).
6. Sudden cliff-edge cut: complete silence (*fermata secco*).
7. Drop-off into *Presto con fuoco* Coda at G minor!
